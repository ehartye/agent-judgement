# Perspectives

Thoughtful people disagree about how to decide. Each view below is right about something. Use it
when its condition holds.

| View | Holds that | Use it when | Ask |
| ---- | ---------- | ----------- | --- |
| **Experienced intuition** | Experts read patterns they cannot put into words | The person has had many tries with quick, clear feedback | What are you noticing, and when were you last wrong about it? |
| **Checks on intuition** | Confidence is a poor guide, and noise is everywhere | The situation is new, rare or slow to answer back | What would we have said without knowing the favourite? |
| **Outside view** | Look at what happened to others first | There is a group of similar cases | Of ten like this, how many went well? |
| **Decision analysis** | Separate what you want from what you expect | The stakes justify the work and the numbers can be found | What is each outcome worth, and how likely? |
| **Robust planning** | Choose what holds up across futures | Nobody can agree on the odds | Where does each option break? |
| **Simple rules** | A few good cues beat a big model | There is little data and things keep shifting | If we could only look at one thing? |
| **Conflict solving** | Some trade-offs dissolve when you separate them | There is evidence that two aims truly clash | Must both be true at the same time and place? |
| **Dissent** | A real disagreement shows what agreement hides | Everyone agreed quickly | Who sees it differently, and what do they know? |
| **Independent judgement** | Several separate views averaged beat one talked-out view | People know different things | What did each of us think before we spoke? |
| **Process design** | A fixed order protects a decision from mood and rank | The decision will be repeated or defended later | What did we agree to look at before we started? |
| **Learning by doing** | Act small, see, adjust | It is easy to undo | What is the smallest step that would teach us? |

## The case for judgement engines

*This is a seller's argument, included because it states a position well.*

As analysis gets cheap, judgement becomes the scarce thing. Judgement means deciding what matters and
what to do when the evidence is incomplete, aims conflict and no rule settles it. On this view, some
would use AI to capture how their best people decide, some to search far more options than a team
could, and some to govern which ideas survive: framing first, testing hard, recording why, and being
willing to say there is not enough to decide. Judging something to be sound is kept apart from having
the authority to do it. (Crucible Agentics, "McKinsey, HBR, and Crucible", November 2026.)

**What this guide takes from it:** frame first, let "not yet" be an answer, look for a real conflict
before solving one, and write down what would reopen the choice.

**What this guide adds:** each person's own view before anyone else's, forecasts that can be marked,
and a date to come back and see.

## When two views clash

Do not blend them. Say which condition holds here, use that view, and record the other as an
objection.
