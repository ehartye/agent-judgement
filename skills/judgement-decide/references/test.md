# Test

Try to break each live option, the favourite hardest.

## 1. Argue every side

For each live option give the strongest case for it and the strongest case against it, at the same
length. Then ask each person for the objection that worries them most. Record every objection with
who raised it, which option it is against, a state, and the cheapest test that would settle it.

An objection is `answered` only when evidence settles it; write that evidence in `settledBy`. One
that stands stays `standing` and goes to the commitment. When real evidence does arrive, change the
verdict and say what changed it; holding firm against a good reason is as bad as giving way to a poor
one. Take a point through two exchanges at most.
If it is still open, name the test and move on.

## 2. Find the real tension

Look for two criteria that pull apart. Ask for the evidence that they truly conflict. They may
not, and that is a result. When they do, try to separate them:

| By | Ask |
| -- | --- |
| time | Could one come first and the other later? |
| place | Could each hold in a different place? |
| condition | Could each hold under a different condition? |
| scale | Does it vanish at a smaller or larger size? |
| role | Could someone else carry one side? |

If none works it is a trade-off. Choose, and record what was given up.

## 3. Tell the failure story

It is a year from now and the favourite went badly. Each person writes how, alone, then shares. For
the two most likely, record the first sign and a guard.

## 4. Your own view (standard and deep)

Now you may give one. Form it without their leanings: start a fresh subagent with the question,
criteria, options and evidence, leave out every take and rating, and ask for its choice, a percentage
and the fact that would change it. Record it as a take with `actor: "model"`. With no subagent
available, skip this step. Show it beside the people's first views, with where it differs and why. Its percentage is a note for
the record. Never let a model's stated confidence settle anything.

For deep decisions, you may also ask a different vendor's model. However many models agree, count about two votes at most.

## 5. Check that it holds

Look at the options in reverse order, and again with the wording made flat. Change the least certain fact by a realistic amount. Say what
flips the choice. If small changes flip it, it is close, and the easier one to undo wins.
